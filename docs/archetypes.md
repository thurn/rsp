# Rogue Spades: archetypes

Rogue Spades has 15 core deck archetypes. Each is built on one pair of the six
colored resonances described in [resonance.md](resonance.md), and every pair
belongs to exactly one archetype. Gray utility sigils support all of them.

Each archetype below lists its game plan, its **enablers** (sigils that set up
the plan), its **payoffs** (sigils that turn the plan into points or gold), the
**threats** opponents pose and how the deck answers them, and its most useful
**splashes**.

| [Spade Master](#spade-master) | Red + Green | Hoard spades, open voids, and trump everything. |
| [Kingmaker](#kingmaker) | Red + Teal | Build winners and hand them to your partner. |
| [Contract Attacker](#contract-attacker) | Red + Purple | Set the opponents' contract, or bury them in bags. |
| [Bonus Chaser](#bonus-chaser) | Red + Orange | Make every played card and side quest pay. |
| [Diamond Flood](#diamond-flood) | Orange + Green | Fill the hand with diamonds and cash them for points and gold. |
| [Gold Miner](#gold-miner) | Orange + Blue | Earn gold, build a stronger collection, and convert gold to points late. |
| [Swap Meet](#swap-meet) | Orange + Teal | Trade constantly and profit from every exchange. |
| [Blind Bidder](#blind-bidder) | Orange + Purple | Bid blind nil as often as possible and survive the deal. |

The three opposed pairs each form an archetype that resolves their tension:
Red and Purple (winning and losing) form Contract Attacker, Orange and Blue
(spending and saving) form Gold Miner, and Green and Teal (keeping and giving)
form Heart Chorus.

## High Card

**Red + Blue.** Win tricks with the biggest card of the led suit.

**Game plan.** Red pushes the hand's ranks upward until it holds several
effective aces. The deck bids high, leads its winners, and collects
trick-winning rewards. Its natural enemy is trump, so Blue controls when and
whether opponents can trump and shows where the danger lies.

**Enablers.**

- Red rank increases and ace and king affinities.
- Blue reveals that expose opponents' voids before a winner is led.
- Blue rule setters that restrict trumping.
- Blue while-held effects, which reward holding a winner until the led suit is
  safe.

**Payoffs.**

- Red "when this card wins a trick" contract value.
- Red rewards for winning without trump.
- Red rewards for high contracts, which a hand full of winners can bid
  confidently.

**Threats and answers.**

- *Opponents trump your winners.* Lead aces early, before opponents develop
  voids, and use Blue trump restrictions for the late tricks.
- *Opponents lower your ranks.* Rank bonuses that overshoot ace act as a
  buffer, because modifiers clamp once after summing: a king at +5 still plays
  as an ace after a −3 debuff.
- *Too many winners produce bags.* Blue bid adjustment raises the bid once the
  hand's strength is clear, and Gray bag relief absorbs the rest.

**Splashes.** Purple rank lowering on opponents' cards; Gray bag relief.

## Spade Master

**Red + Green.** Hoard spades, open voids, and trump everything.

**Game plan.** Green adds spades to the hand and converts short side suits into
spades, which both lengthens the trump suit and opens voids. Once void, the deck
trumps whatever the opponents lead. Red raises spade ranks so the deck also wins
trump-against-trump fights.

**Enablers.**

- Green conversion to spades, and extra spades in the
  starting deck.
- Green void and long-suit rewards, which pay for the same hand shape the deck
  already wants.
- Red rank increases on spades.

**Payoffs.**

- Red trick-winning triggers, conditioned on spades or trumping.
- Green points for playing spades.
- Red rewards for high contracts.

**Threats and answers.**

- *Opponents overtrump.* Red rank increases keep the top spades on top.
- *Opponents lead spades to pull trump.* A long spade suit survives several
  spade leads with trumps to spare.
- *Opponents restrict trumping with Blue rule setters.* Purple sigil disabling
  answers a known rule setter, and Blue's own permission to lead spades early
  lets the deck take control of the lead.
- *Frequent trump wins produce bags.* Bid high with Red contract rewards, and
  use Gray bag relief.

**Splashes.** Purple off-suit play triggers, which count every trump; Blue
permission to lead unbroken spades; Gray bag relief.

## Kingmaker

**Red + Teal.** Build winners and hand them to your partner.

**Game plan.** Red builds high cards and spades; Teal passes them across the
table. The partner wins the tricks, and Kingmaker's sigils reward the partner's
wins. Engravings travel with passed cards, so a passed winner brings its
trick-winning triggers to the partner, and contract value from either seat
improves the shared contract.

**Enablers.**

- Red rank increases on cards the deck plans to pass.
- Teal partner passes before and after bidding.
- Teal effects that strengthen the partner's cards directly.

**Payoffs.**

- Teal rewards when the partner wins a trick.
- Red trick-winning triggers on cards that end up in the partner's hand.
- Red rewards for high contracts, which the partner bids with a strengthened
  hand.

**Threats and answers.**

- *The partner's hand is unknown.* Blue partner-information effects show what
  the partner needs, and post-bid passes let the deck react to the partner's
  bid.
- *Opponents trump the partner's winners.* Pass spades along with high cards.
- *Opponents steal or lower the passed cards.* Pass after bidding, late enough
  that opponents have committed their own effects.

**Splashes.** Blue partner information; Green conversion to spades. Kingmaker
is strongest beside a partner running High Card or Spade Master.

## Contract Attacker

**Red + Purple.** Set the opponents' contract, or bury them in bags.

**Game plan.** The deck attacks the opponents' score directly. Its main line
takes more tricks than the opponents can afford: Red raises its own cards while
Purple lowers theirs, forces their discards, and disables their sigils. Its
second line feeds a conservative partnership overtricks and punishes their bags.
Because a failed contract loses its base value times every multiplier, the
attack grows more damaging as the opponents' collections grow stronger.

**Enablers.**

- Red rank increases and Purple rank lowering on opponents' cards.
- Purple forced discards and sigil disabling.
- Blue reveals, which show where the opponents' tricks will come from.

**Payoffs.**

- Purple rewards when opponents miss their contract.
- Purple point loss for opponents, including heavier bag penalties.
- Red trick-winning triggers from the extra tricks the attack takes.

**Threats and answers.**

- *The attack produces bags.* Gray bag relief absorbs overtricks, and a high
  bid turns extra tricks into contract value.
- *Opponents bid conservatively.* Switch to the bagging line: feed them tricks
  and let Purple's bag penalties do the damage.
- *Opponents bid nil.* Lead low cards through the nil bidder, and aim rank
  lowering at the nil bidder's partner.

**Splashes.** Gray bag relief; Blue reveals.

## Bonus Chaser

**Red + Orange.** Make every played card and side quest pay.

**Game plan.** The deck assembles many small, one-shot rewards: points when
cards are played, side quests that pay for specific accomplishments, and
triggers when cards win. Additive contract value is earned only when the
contract is made, so the deck bids safely and treats its bonuses as the real
score.

**Enablers.**

- Orange side quests and "when played" effects.
- Orange re-triggers and doubling of "when played" effects.
- Red rank increases that make trick-winning triggers reliable.

**Payoffs.**

- Orange one-shot points and side-quest rewards.
- Red "when this card wins a trick" contract value.

**Threats and answers.**

- *A failed contract loses every bonus.* Bid below the hand's strength, use
  Blue bid adjustment late in the round, and absorb the resulting bags with Gray
  bag relief.
- *Opponents disable key sigils.* Spread value across many small sigils so no
  single loss is decisive.
- *Opponents avoid the suits a side quest needs.* Green suit conversion and Red
  lead stealing let the deck choose the suits in play.

**Splashes.** Teal returning played cards, which replays their "when played"
effects; Green suit conversion; Gray bag relief.

## Diamond Flood

**Orange + Green.** Fill the hand with diamonds and cash them for points and
gold.

**Game plan.** Green converts cards to diamonds and rewards a long diamond suit.
Orange makes every diamond pay in gold as well as points. The gold feeds Gray
shop tools, so the deck improves its collection faster than its rivals.

**Enablers.**

- Green conversion to diamonds and creation of diamonds.
- Green long-suit rewards.
- Gray shop tools, which turn extra gold into better offers.

**Payoffs.**

- Red and Green rewards for winning or playing diamonds.
- Orange gold when diamonds are played or win.

**Threats and answers.**

- *Opponents run out of diamonds and trump them.* A longer diamond suit empties
  opponents' diamonds sooner, so lead the top diamonds early, splash Red rank
  increases so they cash first, and splash Blue trump restrictions for the late
  tricks.
- *Opponents hold the top diamonds.* Volume wins: a flooded suit outlasts a
  few high cards, and Red rank increases contest the top.
- *Converted side suits leave voids.* Voids let the deck trump or discard, and
  Green void rewards pay for them.

**Splashes.** Red rank increases; Blue trump restrictions; Gray shop tools.

## Gold Miner

**Orange + Blue.** Earn gold, build a stronger collection, and convert gold to
points late.

**Game plan.** Orange generates bonus gold and raises the interest cap. Blue's
while-held effects let cards earn gold every trick they stay in hand. Early in
the run the gold buys rarer sigils through Gray shop tools; late in the run,
Orange converts the surplus into points.

**Enablers.**

- Orange gold when played, while held, or on a condition, and gold
  multipliers.
- Orange higher interest cap.
- Blue while-held effects.
- Gray shop tools.

**Payoffs.**

- Orange conversion of gold to points.
- Rare and uncommon sigils bought with surplus gold, from any resonance.

**Threats and answers.**

- *Gold arrives too late to matter.* Buy economy early, spend on the
  collection in the middle of the run, and convert to points near the end.
- *Following suit forces while-held cards out of hand.* Gray engraving control
  places while-held sigils on cards in long suits, where other cards can follow
  instead.
- *Opponents steal cards or force discards.* Spread income across several
  sigils rather than one large earner.
- *The partnership's score lags while the deck invests.* Gold Miner pairs well
  with a partner running a points-focused archetype, and eventually both
  partners benefit from its stronger collection.

**Splashes.** Gray shop tools; Green suit flexibility.

## Swap Meet

**Orange + Teal.** Trade constantly and profit from every exchange.

**Game plan.** Teal passes and swaps cards with the partner, the opponents, and
the discard pile. Orange and Teal pass triggers pay for each completed trade.
Every exchange also improves the hand, so the deck reshapes a mediocre deal
into the hand it wants.

**Enablers.**

- Teal passes to the partner at every window.
- Teal swaps with opponents and with played or discarded cards.
- Blue reveals, which show which cards to take.

**Payoffs.**

- Teal pass triggers.
- Orange gold for each completed trade.

**Threats and answers.**

- *Swaps with opponents hand them good cards.* Send away cards the deck has no
  use for, and use Blue reveals to choose what to take.
- *Exchanges need cards remaining in hand.* Trade early in the round, when hands
  are full.
- *Opponents disable pass triggers.* Keep trades valuable in themselves, so each
  one improves the hand even without its reward.

**Splashes.** Blue reveals; Gray card selection. Swap Meet supports any partner,
especially Kingmaker and Nil Guard, whose plans depend on exchanges.

## Blind Bidder

**Orange + Purple.** Bid blind nil as often as possible and survive the deal.

**Game plan.** A blind nil is worth ±200 points and 200 gold on success. Purple
lowers the blind nil threshold so the deck qualifies more often, then lowers its
own ranks after the deal to survive whatever it was dealt. Orange rewards the
gamble itself.

**Enablers.**

- Purple reduction of the blind nil threshold.
- Purple rank lowering on your own cards.
- Teal passes that send dangerous cards to the partner.

**Payoffs.**

- Purple increased blind nil and nil value.
- Orange rewards for bidding blind.
- Purple "when you lose a trick" triggers.

**Threats and answers.**

- *Success removes eligibility.* A successful blind nil pulls the partnership
  closer to the lead, so the deck alternates blind nil with ordinary nil and
  relies on threshold reduction to qualify again.
- *The deal contains aces.* Lower their ranks with Purple, pass them to the
  partner with Teal, and discard them on voids.
- *Opponents lead low to force a win.* Aggressive rank lowering keeps the
  deck's cards beneath theirs; a partner covering with high cards finishes the
  job.

**Splashes.** Teal partner passes. Blind Bidder is strongest beside a Nil Guard
partner.

## While Held

**Green + Blue.** Keep powerful cards in hand for as many tricks as possible.

**Game plan.** Blue's while-held effects pay each trick they remain in hand.
Hands usually empty over the round, so the goal is to keep the sigil-bearing
cards until the final tricks while other cards satisfy follow-suit obligations.
Green supplies those other cards and the suit flexibility to play them. Extra
cards from Green creation or Teal pick-ups let the deck finish the round still
holding a sigil card, which keeps its effect through scoring.

**Enablers.**

- Green creation of cards in hand, which gives the deck extra cards to follow
  with.
- Green suit conversion and cards that count as any suit.
- Gray engraving control, which places while-held sigils on cards unlikely to
  be forced out.

**Payoffs.**

- Blue while-held effects and while-held multipliers.
- Blue bonuses for the last card played in a round.

**Threats and answers.**

- *Following suit forces a sigil card out.* Keep enough other cards of each suit,
  and use cards that count as any suit to follow instead.
- *Opponents force discards or steal cards.* Spread while-held value across
  several cards.
- *Opponents lead the suit of a sigil card repeatedly.* Teal returning played
  cards to hand brings a forced-out sigil card back.

**Splashes.** Teal returning played cards; Gray engraving control.

## Heart Chorus

**Green + Teal.** Grow a hearts suit that strengthens as the round goes on.

**Game plan.** Green converts cards to hearts and makes hearts grow stronger as
the round progresses. Teal brings played hearts back into hand for another turn
and consolidates the partnership's hearts in the hand that can use them. The
suit starts modest and dominates the late tricks.

**Enablers.**

- Green conversion to hearts.
- Green growth effects, which raise hearts' ranks as tricks pass.
- Teal returning played cards to hand, and partner passes of hearts.

**Payoffs.**

- Red and Green rewards for winning or playing hearts.
- Green growth effects, which make the late-round hearts nearly unbeatable.

**Threats and answers.**

- *Hearts are weak early.* Play off-suit cards and low hearts in the first
  tricks, and hold the growing hearts for later.
- *Opponents run out of hearts and trump them.* Returned hearts refill the suit,
  and Blue trump restrictions protect the late tricks.
- *Opponents lower heart ranks.* Growth continues through the round and outpaces
  one-time debuffs.

**Splashes.** Blue trump restrictions; Red rank increases.

## Discard Dominance

**Green + Purple.** Open voids and profit from every off-suit discard.

**Game plan.** Green converts short suits away, opening voids. On a void suit,
the deck discards freely, and Purple pays for each discard. Purple also lets the
deck skip following suit, so it discards even when it holds the led suit. The
deck takes few tricks, so it pairs naturally with a low contract or a nil.

**Enablers.**

- Green suit conversion and void rewards.
- Purple permission to skip following suit.
- Teal passes that send away an entire short suit.

**Payoffs.**

- Purple rewards for discarding off suit.
- Purple rewards for low contracts.
- Purple off-suit play triggers, which also count trumps.

**Threats and answers.**

- *Opponents stop leading the void suit.* Open several voids, so most leads
  create a discard, and use skip-follow permissions on the rest.
- *Discards lose tricks the contract needs.* Bid low, or bid nil and let
  Purple's nil payoffs join the discard rewards.
- *Opponents force the deck to take tricks by leading its long suit.* Lower the
  long suit's ranks with Purple, or trump in from a void when a trick is worth
  taking.

**Splashes.** Teal passes; Blue bid adjustment.

## Exact Contractor

**Blue + Teal.** Take exactly the bid, and get paid for precision.

**Game plan.** Blue's exact-bid rewards are among the strongest multipliers in
the game, conditioned on the partnership taking exactly its bid. The deck
gathers information before bidding, adjusts the bid as the round develops, and
chooses whether each card wins or loses. Teal moves winners between partners so
the partnership lands on its number.

**Enablers.**

- Blue reveals before bidding and during play.
- Blue bid adjustment after the bid is declared.
- Blue choices of direction, such as setting a card high or low.
- Teal partner passes during play.

**Payoffs.**

- Blue rewards for making exactly the bid.
- Blue rewards for making the individual bid.

**Threats and answers.**

- *Opponents dump extra tricks on the partnership.* Choice-of-direction effects
  and Purple rank lowering turn a forced winner into a loser.
- *Opponents set the contract instead.* Bid adjustment lowers the bid when the
  hand turns out weaker than expected.
- *Opponents change ranks mid-round.* Reveals show the new ranks before the deck
  commits a card.

**Splashes.** Purple rank lowering and "when you lose a trick" triggers; Gray
card selection.

## Nil Champion

**Blue + Purple.** Bid nil and scale the value of a successful nil.

**Game plan.** Nil scores a flat value, so the deck grows it through Purple
nil-value sigils. Purple lowers the deck's own ranks until the hand holds no
winners. Blue shows where danger lies and when a dangerous card can be discarded
safely. A nil bidder's contract sigils still modify the partner's contract, so
the deck can carry contract sigils for its partner as well.

**Enablers.**

- Purple rank lowering on your own cards.
- Blue reveals, which show whether a trick is safe to duck.
- Blue choices of direction, which set a card low when needed.

**Payoffs.**

- Purple increased nil value.
- Purple "when you lose a trick" triggers.

**Threats and answers.**

- *Opponents lead low cards to force a win.* Lower ranks aggressively; the
  deck's cards need to sit below the opponents' cards of the same suit.
- *Tied ranks favor the later card.* Playing last with a tied rank wins the
  trick, so the deck prefers to play its lowest cards early in a trick and keeps
  a strictly lower card for last position.
- *The deal holds unavoidable winners.* Teal passes send them to the partner,
  and voids let the deck discard them.

**Splashes.** Teal partner passes; Green void creation.

## Nil Guard

**Teal + Purple.** Make your partner's nil succeed.

**Game plan.** The partner bids nil, and the deck protects that nil. Teal
exchanges take the partner's dangerous high cards and give back safe low cards.
Purple lowers the partner's ranks and rewards the partner's successful nil.
Receiving the partner's high cards also strengthens the deck's own contract,
which it makes alone.

**Enablers.**

- Teal partner passes, before and after bidding.
- Teal effects that target the partner's cards.
- Purple rank lowering, aimed at the partner's hand.

**Payoffs.**

- Purple increased nil value, aimed at the partner's nil.
- Teal rewards tied to the partner's play.

**Threats and answers.**

- *Opponents lead the partner's weak suit.* Cover with high cards, winning the
  trick above the partner's card; Red rank increases make the cover reliable.
- *The partner's hand is unknown.* Blue partner-information effects show which
  cards to take.
- *Opponents target the deck's own contract.* The high cards received from the
  partner make the contract easier to fulfil.

**Splashes.** Red rank increases; Blue partner information. Nil Guard is
strongest beside a Nil Champion or Blind Bidder partner.
