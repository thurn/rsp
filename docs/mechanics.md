# Rogue Spades: sigil mechanics

This document catalogs the mechanical families that sigils draw from. Each
family lists:

- **Primary** resonance: the resonance that owns the family and carries its
  strongest and most common versions.
- **Secondary** resonances: resonances that use the family in narrower or
  conditioned forms.
- **Most interested:** the archetypes that want the family most, with core
  archetypes first and splashing archetypes after.
- **Variations:** the ways the family can be expressed on individual sigils.

Every sigil effect is ongoing or triggered, as defined in
[rules-text.md](rules-text.md). Each variation below is expressed through a
timing window: always on, before or after bidding, while held, when played, or
when a trick is won or lost.

See [resonance.md](resonance.md) for each resonance's identity and
[archetypes.md](archetypes.md) for each archetype's game plan. Sigil timing,
engraving, exchange, and scoring rules are defined in
[game-overview.md](game-overview.md).

## Rank

### Raising your ranks

**Primary:** Red. **Secondary:** Green (growth over the round), Teal (raising
your partner's cards).

**Most interested:** High Card, Spade Master, Kingmaker, Contract Attacker;
splashed by Heart Chorus, Diamond Flood, and Nil Guard.

Higher cards win more tricks. This is the most broadly useful family in the
game, so its unconditional versions are modest.

**Variations.**

- *Intrinsic:* the engraved card gains +N rank for the round.
- *Aura:* while this card is held, cards of a suit or of your longest suit gain
  +N rank.
- *Targeted:* when played or revealed, choose cards in hand to raise.
- *Rank-specific:* affinities, bonuses, and rewards tied to aces and kings,
  such as kings that play as aces or rewards for each ace held at the deal.
- *Overshoot:* modifiers clamp once after summing, so bonuses that push a card
  past ace act as a buffer against rank lowering.
- *Ties:* this card wins ties, whatever its position in the trick.

### Lowering your ranks

**Primary:** Purple. **Secondary:** Blue (choosing to lower a card when
needed).

**Most interested:** Nil Champion, Blind Bidder, Nil Guard (aimed at the
partner's hand); splashed by Exact Contractor and Discard Dominance.

Lower cards lose tricks, which nil decks and precision decks need.

**Variations.**

- *Intrinsic:* the engraved card loses N rank, or its rank is set to two.
- *Post-bid:* after bidding, lower cards in hand, which rescues a nil bid made
  on a risky hand.
- *Low-card rewards:* rewards for holding, playing, or losing with twos and
  other low cards.
- *Ties:* this card loses ties, whatever its position in the trick.

### Changing opponents' ranks

**Primary:** Purple. **Secondary:** Red.

**Most interested:** Contract Attacker, High Card, Kingmaker, and Spade Master
(lowering); Nil Champion, Blind Bidder, and Contract Attacker's bagging line
(raising).

Lowering opponents' cards makes your winners safer. Raising them feeds the
opponents tricks: their higher cards overtake a nil bidder's cards, and extra
tricks become bags.

**Variations.**

- *Targeting:* the opponents' highest card, random cards, a named suit, or
  every card of the led suit.
- *Duration:* this trick, while this card is held, or for the round.
- *Condition:* only against a partnership that bid above a threshold, or only
  against a nil bidder's partner.

### Rank choice and swapping

**Primary:** Blue. **Secondary:** Red, Purple.

**Most interested:** Exact Contractor, Nil Champion, High Card; splashed by
Contract Attacker and Blind Bidder.

Letting the player choose a card's rank at the moment it matters serves decks
that want to win and decks that want to lose, and serves precision decks most.

**Variations.**

- *Ace direction:* choose whether an ace plays as the highest or lowest rank.
- *Set on play:* when played, set this card's rank to two or ace.
- *Rank swapping:* exchange the ranks of two cards in hand, or of a card in hand
  and a card in an opponent's hand.
- *Bounded choice:* change a card's rank by up to N in either direction.
- *Wild rank:* a joker or wild card whose rank is chosen when it is played.

### Changing played cards

**Primary:** Blue. **Secondary:** Red, Purple.

**Most interested:** Exact Contractor, Nil Guard, Contract Attacker; splashed by
High Card and Nil Champion.

These effects target cards already played to the current trick, changing its
winner after other players have committed. Each such sigil states that it can
target a played card; legality of earlier plays stands.

**Variations.**

- *Lower a winner:* when played, lower a card already in the trick, handing
  the trick to another card.
- *Raise a card:* raise your partner's played card so it wins, or raise an
  opponent's card to take a trick away from a nil bidder.
- *Suit change:* change a played card's suit, making it trump or removing it
  from contention.
- *Last word:* this card, played fourth, may transform one played card.

### Reversing rank order

**Primary:** Purple. **Secondary:** Blue.

**Most interested:** Nil Champion, Discard Dominance, Contract Attacker.

Under "lowest wins," the lowest effective rank of the eligible suit wins the
trick, as defined in the core rules. Reversal turns a hand of low cards into
winners and a hand of high cards into safe losers.

**Variations.**

- *Scope:* one trick, one suit, or the whole round.
- *Trigger:* when this card is led, when a condition is met, or at the start of
  play.
- *Asymmetry:* reversal applies only to tricks that a named suit leads.

### Growth over the round

**Primary:** Green. **Secondary:** Red.

**Most interested:** Heart Chorus, While Held; splashed by Spade Master and
Diamond Flood.

Cards grow stronger as the round progresses, trading early weakness for
late-round dominance.

**Variations.**

- *Thresholds:* after N tricks, cards of a suit gain +N rank.
- *Counters:* each card of a suit played raises the remaining cards of that
  suit.
- *Outcome counters:* each trick won or lost adds to a growing bonus.
- *Reset:* growth resets each round, keeping the pattern fresh on every deal.

## Suit

### Changing your suits

**Primary:** Green. **Secondary:** Blue (choosing the target at play).

**Most interested:** Diamond Flood, Heart Chorus, Spade Master, Discard
Dominance, While Held; splashed by Kingmaker and Bonus Chaser.

Suit conversion builds long suits and opens voids at the same time. Converted
cards fully belong to their new suit.

**Variations.**

- *Intrinsic:* the engraved card becomes a named suit.
- *Directional:* before bidding, convert N cards of your shortest suit to your
  longest suit.
- *Targeted:* when played or revealed, choose cards in hand and change their
  suit.
- *Suit swapping:* every card of one suit in your hand becomes a second suit,
  and every card of the second suit becomes the first.
- *Spades:* conversion to spades builds trump length; conversion away from
  spades protects a nil.

### Wild suits

**Primary:** Green. **Secondary:** Blue.

**Most interested:** While Held, Exact Contractor, Heart Chorus; splashed by
Nil Champion.

A wild card belongs to whichever suit its holder needs, which keeps other cards
in hand and makes every trick flexible.

**Variations.**

- *Any suit:* the engraved card counts as any suit, chosen when played.
- *Optional follow:* the card may count as the led suit, so its holder chooses
  between following and discarding.
- *Jokers:* create a joker in hand with a chosen suit and rank.
- *Void tension:* a card that always counts as the led suit prevents voids, so
  Discard Dominance prefers the optional version.

### Changing opponents' suits

**Primary:** Purple. **Secondary:** Green.

**Most interested:** Contract Attacker, High Card, Spade Master.

Changing opponents' suits strips their trumps or breaks their voids.

**Variations.**

- *Disarm:* convert an opponent's spades to another suit, removing trumps.
- *Fill voids:* give an opponent a card of a suit they lack, so they follow
  instead of trumping.
- *Random:* convert random cards in an opponent's hand.

### Trump replacement

**Primary:** Blue. **Secondary:** Green.

**Most interested:** Diamond Flood and Heart Chorus (their suit becomes trump),
High Card (no trump at all); Spade Master uses this family to defend spades.

Replacing trump rewrites the round around a chosen suit. Each such sigil states
whether it also changes lead restrictions and trump classification.

**Variations.**

- *Longest suit:* your longest suit becomes trump for the round.
- *No trump:* no suit is trump for a trick or a round.
- *Delayed:* trump changes after trick N.
- *Conditional:* trump changes only while this card is held.

## Hand and deck

### Voids, singletons, and long suits

**Primary:** Green. **Secondary:** Purple.

**Most interested:** Discard Dominance, Spade Master, Diamond Flood, Heart
Chorus.

A void and a long suit are two views of the same hand shape: thirteen cards
spread over fewer suits. Singletons become voids after one play.

**Variations.**

- *Deal-time:* rewards based on hand shape at the start of play, after
  conversions.
- *Ongoing:* rewards for each trick played while void in a suit.
- *Long-suit thresholds:* rewards for holding five or more cards of a suit.
- *Singletons:* rewards for playing a singleton, or effects that make a
  singleton the strongest card of its suit.

### Creating cards

**Primary:** Green (specific cards). **Secondary:** Orange (random cards).

**Most interested:** While Held, Diamond Flood, Heart Chorus; Bonus Chaser for
random creation.

Created cards transform existing cards or join the hand as extra cards.

**Variations.**

- *Specific:* transform a card in hand into a named card, such as the ace of
  your longest suit.
- *Random:* transform cards into random cards, occasionally producing strong
  results.
- *Copies:* create a copy of a card already in hand, including its suit and
  rank.
- *Additions:* add a created card to your hand, giving you an extra card for
  the round.

### Opening hand control

**Primary:** Green. **Secondary:** Gray.

**Most interested:** Spade Master, Diamond Flood, Heart Chorus, High Card.

Opening hand control shapes the deal itself.

**Variations.**

- *Forced cards:* a named card, such as the ace of spades, is dealt to you when
  it is available.
- *Extra cards:* receive N extra created cards at the deal.
- *Redeal:* before bidding, replace N cards in hand with random cards.

### Hand repair

**Primary:** Gray.

**Most interested:** every archetype; High Card and Spade Master most, and nil
archetypes in reverse.

Hand repair sets a floor on the deal, reducing the swing of a bad hand.

**Variations.**

- *Deficiency trigger:* if the hand holds no spades, replace a card with a
  spade; if it holds no face cards, replace a card with a face card.
- *Reverse repair:* if the hand holds too many high cards for a nil, replace
  one with a low card.
- *Threshold:* repair triggers when a hand scores below a strength threshold.

### Deck construction

**Primary:** Green. **Secondary:** Orange (randomization).

**Most interested:** Spade Master, Diamond Flood, Heart Chorus.

Deck construction introduces a persistent deck layer across rounds. The core
rules deal a fresh standard deck each round, so these mechanics need a rule
defining how the deck carries changes forward. The deck is shared by all four
seats, so broad changes reach opponents' deals too.

**Variations.**

- *Additions:* add extra spades or duplicates of a suit to the starting deck.
- *Jokers:* add jokers to the deck as the two highest trumps.
- *Removals:* remove low cards or a suit's small cards.
- *Permanent modification:* at the end of each round, permanently raise a
  card's rank or change its suit.
- *Randomization:* remove random cards or add random duplicates each round.
- *Owned cards:* cards added by your sigil are always dealt to you.

### Card selection

**Primary:** Gray. **Secondary:** Teal, Green.

**Most interested:** every archetype; Exact Contractor and Swap Meet most.

Card selection lets a player find the card the hand is missing.

**Variations.**

- *Look and keep:* before bidding, look at 3 created cards and swap one into
  your hand.
- *Discard swaps:* when you play this card, swap a card in hand with a card
  from a completed trick (see
  Recursion).
- *Shop cards:* buy specific cards in the shop for your next opening hand.

## Moving cards

### Recursion

**Primary:** Teal.

**Most interested:** Heart Chorus, Bonus Chaser, Swap Meet; splashed by While
Held and Gold Miner.

Recursion brings played cards back into hand in exchange for a card in hand,
which replays their effects or restores a card the deck needs.

**Variations.**

- *Your cards:* when you play this card, swap a card in hand with a card you
  played earlier this round.
- *Opponents' cards:* when you win a trick, swap a card in hand with a card an
  opponent played to it,
  taking over its engraving.
- *Any played card:* when you lose a trick, swap with any card from a completed
  trick.
- *Triggered:* recursion fires when you lose a trick or when a suit is led.

### Partner exchange

**Primary:** Teal. **Secondary:** Red (strengthening passed cards), Purple
(weakening a nil partner's cards).

**Most interested:** Kingmaker, Nil Guard, Nil Champion, Blind Bidder, Exact
Contractor, Swap Meet; splashed by Discard Dominance and Spade Master.

Partner exchange is the most widely shared mechanic in the game. One exchange
serves opposite plans: it sends winners to a trick-taking partner or takes them
away from a nil partner.

**Variations.**

- *Before bidding:* both partners bid with improved hands.
- *After bidding:* the exchange rescues a nil or strengthens a contract after
  commitments.
- *During play:* exchanges triggered by a won or lost trick resolve before the
  next trick and react to how the round develops.
- *Selection:* each partner chooses their own cards, or one partner chooses from
  the other's hand.
- *Triggers:* completed passes pay rewards to one or both partners.

### Opponent exchange and theft

**Primary:** Teal. **Secondary:** Purple.

**Most interested:** Swap Meet, Contract Attacker; splashed by High Card and
Spade Master.

Taking cards from opponents weakens them and strengthens you in one move. An
engraved card brings its sigil with it, so theft can capture an opponent's
effects for the round. Theft is one-way, leaving the thief with an extra card
and the victim a card short; a swap trades cards evenly.

**Variations.**

- *Chosen theft:* after bidding, take a chosen card, which pairs with reveals.
- *Random theft:* when you win a trick, take a random card, cheaper and less
  reliable.
- *Swaps:* before bidding, exchange a card with an opponent, choosing what you
  give.
- *Directional passing:* every seat passes cards to its left before bidding or
  before play, in the style of Hearts.

### Forced discards

**Primary:** Purple. **Secondary:** Orange (random discards).

**Most interested:** Contract Attacker; splashed by High Card and Discard
Dominance.

Forced discards remove key cards from opponents' hands, leaving them a card
short.

**Variations.**

- *Opponents:* each opponent discards a card of the victim's choice, the
  controller's choice, or at random.
- *All players:* every seat discards, which favors the deck that planned for
  it.
- *Timing:* before bidding (disrupting bids) or after bidding (disrupting
  contracts).
- *Replacement:* the replacement is random, a low card, or a card of a named
  suit.

## Rules of play

### Following-suit relief

**Primary:** Purple. **Secondary:** Blue.

**Most interested:** Discard Dominance, While Held, Nil Champion; splashed by
Exact Contractor.

Relief from following suit lets a player discard or trump while holding the
led suit.

**Variations.**

- *Card-bound:* this card may be played off suit at any time.
- *Triggered:* after you lose a trick, you may skip following suit on the next
  trick.
- *Suit-bound:* you may decline to follow a named suit.
- *Protective:* while this card is held, it is never required to follow suit.

### Trick restrictions

**Primary:** Blue. **Secondary:** Red, Purple.

**Most interested:** High Card, Nil Guard, Nil Champion, Contract Attacker;
splashed by Spade Master.

Trick restrictions change which cards may be played or can win a single trick.

**Variations.**

- *Block trumping:* no trump may be played to this trick unless its holder has
  only trump.
- *Block aces:* aces cannot win this trick, which protects a nil bidder.
- *Forced plays:* opponents must play a named card, their highest card, or
  their lowest card if able.
- *Overtrumping (Red):* players must overtrump if able, burning opponents'
  high spades.

### Trump and spade-breaking rules

**Primary:** Blue. **Secondary:** Red.

**Most interested:** Spade Master, High Card, Diamond Flood.

These global rule setters govern when spades may be led and when trump may be
played.

**Variations.**

- *Unbroken spades:* you may lead spades before they are broken.
- *Conditional trump:* players cannot trump until their partnership wins a
  trick of a named suit.
- *Timed trump:* trump is unavailable for the first N tricks.

### Playing multiple cards

**Primary:** Red. **Secondary:** Purple.

**Most interested:** High Card, Contract Attacker; Discard Dominance and Nil
Champion for dumping cards.

Playing two cards to one trick concentrates power or sheds a dangerous card.
The extra card leaves its holder a card short, so their hand runs out a trick
early and they skip the final trick.

**Variations.**

- *Support:* when you play this card, a second card from your hand adds its
  rank to it.
- *Choice:* when you play this card, also play a second card and choose which
  one competes.
- *Dump:* when you play this card, also play a second card face up as a
  discard.
- *Replacement:* the extra card is replaced with a created card, keeping the
  holder's hand size.

### Lead control

**Primary:** Red. **Secondary:** Blue, Teal.

**Most interested:** Exact Contractor, Spade Master, Bonus Chaser, Nil Guard;
splashed by High Card.

Controlling the lead controls which suit each trick is about.

**Variations.**

- *Steal:* when you lose a trick, you may take the lead.
- *Starting player:* after bidding, choose who leads the first trick.
- *Hand-off:* when you win a trick, you may give the lead to your partner.

## Bidding and contracts

### Bid adjustment

**Primary:** Blue. **Secondary:** Red (raising), Purple (changing opponents'
contracts).

**Most interested:** Exact Contractor, High Card, Spade Master, Bonus Chaser;
Contract Attacker for opponents' contracts.

Bid adjustment changes a contract after it is declared.

**Variations.**

- *Increase:* raise your bid by one after trick N.
- *Decrease:* after trick N, lower your bid by one, at a cost in points or
  gold.
- *Either:* after trick 6, you may change your bid by one in either direction.
- *Partner:* after trick N, adjust your partner's bid.
- *Opponents:* raise the opponents' contract, making it harder to fulfil.

### Bidding constraints

**Primary:** Blue. **Secondary:** Orange, Purple.

**Most interested:** Exact Contractor, Contract Attacker, Nil Champion.

Bidding constraints are global rule setters that shape everyone's bids.

**Variations.**

- *Sum rule:* the four bids must not total 13, so at least one partnership over-
  or underbids.
- *Nil or big:* every player bids nil or four or more.
- *Half-trick bids:* the first partner to bid may bid in half tricks, and the
  second partner's bid must bring the contract to a whole number.
- *Opponent minimums:* opponents must bid at least N.

### Contract-shape rewards

**Primary:** Blue (exact and individual bids). **Secondary:** Red (high
contracts), Purple (low contracts).

**Most interested:** Exact Contractor, High Card, Spade Master, Discard
Dominance, Bonus Chaser.

These payoffs reward the kind of contract a partnership bids and how it lands.

**Variations.**

- *Exact:* a multiplier or bonus when the partnership takes exactly its bid.
- *Individual:* a bonus when you personally take your own bid.
- *High contracts:* a bonus when the contract is N or more.
- *Low contracts:* a bonus when the contract is N or less.

## Nil and blind bidding

### Nil value

**Primary:** Purple. **Secondary:** Blue, Orange.

**Most interested:** Nil Champion, Blind Bidder, Nil Guard.

Nil keeps a flat value unless a sigil explicitly changes it, so nil decks scale
through this family.

**Variations.**

- *Additive:* +N to your successful nil.
- *Multiplier:* +N× on your nil value, rare and explicit.
- *Partner's nil:* bonuses to your partner's successful nil.
- *Insurance:* a failed nil costs less.
- *Gold:* bonus gold for a successful nil (Orange).

### Blind bidding

**Primary:** Orange. **Secondary:** Purple.

**Most interested:** Blind Bidder; splashed by Nil Champion and Bonus Chaser.

Blind bids are gambles on an unseen hand.

**Variations.**

- *Threshold:* lower the deficit required to bid blind nil (Purple).
- *Access:* allow blind nil whatever the score.
- *Rewards:* extra points or gold for a successful blind bid.
- *Blind contracts:* bid an ordinary contract blind for a larger payout.
- *Blind six:* before seeing its cards, a partnership commits to taking exactly
  six tricks, for a larger payout on an exact result.
- *Peek:* see N cards before deciding whether to bid blind nil, an explicit
  exception to the blind commitment rule, paid at a reduced value.

### Losing tricks

**Primary:** Purple.

**Most interested:** Nil Champion, Nil Guard, Blind Bidder; splashed by
Discard Dominance and Exact Contractor.

Payoffs for losing turn ducked tricks into value.

**Variations.**

- *Per trick:* gain something each time you lose a trick.
- *Ducking:* gain more when you lose while holding a higher card of the led
  suit.
- *Partner:* gain something when you lose a trick your partner wins.

### Opponent denial

**Primary:** Purple. **Secondary:** Red.

**Most interested:** Contract Attacker; splashed by High Card and Spade Master.

Denial rewards damaging the opponents' score. Points awarded or removed outside
the contract say so explicitly.

**Variations.**

- *Set rewards:* a bonus when the opponents miss their contract.
- *Nil breaking:* a bonus when an opposing nil fails.
- *Point loss:* the opponents lose N points.
- *Scaling:* the reward grows with the opponents' bid.

## Information

### Revealing opponents' cards

**Primary:** Blue. **Secondary:** Purple.

**Most interested:** Exact Contractor, Nil Champion, Contract Attacker, High
Card; splashed by Swap Meet.

Information lets a player bid accurately and play at the right moment.

**Variations.**

- *Before bidding:* reveal N random cards from each opponent's hand.
- *During play:* when you win a trick, reveal a random card from an opponent's
  hand.
- *Shape:* reveal an opponent's suit counts or voids.
- *Public reveals:* every player reveals N random cards before bidding.
- *Sigils:* reveal where opponents' sigils are engraved.

### Partner information

**Primary:** Blue. **Secondary:** Teal.

**Most interested:** Kingmaker, Nil Guard, Exact Contractor.

Partner information improves coordination beyond the table talk of bids and
plays.

**Variations.**

- *Counts:* learn your partner's suit counts or number of high cards.
- *Specific cards:* see N cards from your partner's hand.
- *Timing:* before bidding or after bidding.
- *Mutual:* both partners see each other's revealed cards.

## Triggers and timing

### While held

**Primary:** Blue. **Secondary:** Orange (gold while held), Green (support for
holding).

**Most interested:** While Held, Gold Miner, Nil Champion, High Card.

While-held effects apply until their card leaves the hand. Hands usually empty
over the 13 tricks, so while-held payoffs accrue per trick held or at a named
checkpoint. A hand with extra cards can still hold a card after the last
trick, keeping its effect through scoring.

**Variations.**

- *Aura:* other cards gain an effect while this card is held.
- *Per trick:* gain points or gold for each trick this card remains in hand.
- *Checkpoint:* a multiplier applies if this card is held at the start of a
  named trick.
- *Holding support:* effects that extend or protect while-held cards, such as
  releasing them from follow-suit obligations.

### When played and re-triggers

**Primary:** Orange. **Secondary:** Red.

**Most interested:** Bonus Chaser, Gold Miner, Swap Meet.

"When played" effects resolve once per play; re-triggers multiply their value.

**Variations.**

- *When played:* resolve an effect after the card is committed.
- *When led:* resolve an effect only when this card leads a trick.
- *Re-trigger:* the next "when played" effect resolves twice.
- *Doubling:* all your "when played" effects resolve twice this trick.

### Win triggers

**Primary:** Red.

**Most interested:** High Card, Spade Master, Bonus Chaser, Kingmaker.

Win triggers pay after a trick's winner is determined.

**Variations.**

- *This card:* when this card wins a trick.
- *Suit:* when a card of a named suit wins a trick for you.
- *Trump:* when you win with trump, or when you win without trump.
- *Partner:* when your partner wins a trick (Teal).

### Trick sequences

**Primary:** Red. **Secondary:** Blue.

**Most interested:** Spade Master, High Card, Contract Attacker.

Sequence rewards pay for runs of winning.

**Variations.**

- *Streaks:* a reward for each consecutive trick won.
- *Opening tricks:* a reward for winning the first N tricks.
- *Near-sweep:* a reward for winning 12 tricks.
- *Sweep:* a large reward for winning all 13 tricks.

### First and last cards

**Primary:** Red (first). **Secondary:** Blue (last).

**Most interested:** Bonus Chaser, While Held, High Card.

Position-in-round bonuses reward playing a specific card at the start or end.

**Variations.**

- *First card:* a bonus to the first card you play each round.
- *Last card:* a bonus to the last card you play each round.
- *Last of suit:* a bonus to the last card of a suit you hold.

### Trick position

**Primary:** Blue. **Secondary:** Purple.

**Most interested:** Nil Champion, Exact Contractor, High Card.

Trick position triggers care whether a card is played first, second, third, or
fourth to a trick.

**Variations.**

- *Last to play:* a bonus when you play last and win or lose.
- *Leading:* a bonus when you lead and win.
- *Rank by position:* this card gains rank when played fourth.

### Adjacency

**Primary:** Green. **Secondary:** Blue.

**Most interested:** While Held, Heart Chorus.

Adjacency effects use the fixed hand slots defined in the core rules.

**Variations.**

- *Neighbor auras:* cards adjacent to this card gain rank.
- *Neighbor conversion:* adjacent cards take this card's suit.
- *Empty slots:* a bonus when a neighboring slot has been played.

### Costs

**Primary:** Orange. **Secondary:** Purple.

**Most interested:** Bonus Chaser, Gold Miner, Blind Bidder, Contract Attacker.

Costs let a sigil exceed its normal power in exchange for a sacrifice. A cost
is paid as part of a trigger, as in "when you play this card, you may pay 20
gold to …"

**Variations.**

- *Points:* when you play this card, you may pay N points for a powerful
  effect.
- *Gold:* before bidding, you may pay N gold for a powerful effect.
- *Cards:* before bidding, you may replace a high card in hand with a created
  two for a powerful effect.
- *Bags:* when this card wins a trick, take N bags for a powerful effect.

## Scoring

### Suit payoffs

**Primary:** Green (playing a suit). **Secondary:** Red (winning with a suit),
Purple (discarding a suit).

**Most interested:** Diamond Flood, Heart Chorus, Spade Master, Discard
Dominance.

Suit payoffs pay for doing something with a specific suit.

**Variations.**

- *Playing:* contract value for each card of a suit you play.
- *Winning:* contract value for each trick you win with a suit.
- *Discarding:* contract value for each card of a suit you discard off suit.
- *Named or relative:* a named suit, or your longest suit.

### Card-bound points

**Primary:** Orange. **Secondary:** Red.

**Most interested:** Bonus Chaser; splashed by Gold Miner, Diamond Flood, and
Swap Meet.

Card-bound points attach one-shot value to individual cards, paid when those
cards are played or win.

**Variations.**

- *Engraved:* this card pays +N contract value when played.
- *Targeted:* when played or revealed, choose N cards in hand; each pays +N
  contract value when played.
- *On win:* this card pays +N contract value when it wins a trick (Red).
- *Escalating:* each card's payout rises for every card of the same suit
  already played this round.

### Additive contract value

**Primary:** Gray (small, unconditional). **Secondary:** every colored
resonance (conditioned).

**Most interested:** every archetype that bids a contract.

Additive bonuses raise a made contract and vanish on a failed one.

**Variations.**

- *Flat:* +N contract value each round.
- *Per bid trick:* +N contract value per trick bid.
- *During play:* contract value earned from events during the round.
- *Outside the contract:* raw points that score whether or not the contract is
  made, stated explicitly.

### Multipliers

**Primary:** Blue (exact bids and while held). **Secondary:** Red (high
contracts), Purple (nil value).

**Most interested:** Exact Contractor, While Held, High Card, Spade Master.

Multipliers raise the stakes of a contract in both directions and sum rather
than compound. They are uncommon, keeping round scores near the target growth
curve.

**Variations.**

- *Contract:* +N× on the final contract.
- *Conditional:* +N× if a condition is met, inactive on failure.
- *While held:* +N× if this card is held at a checkpoint.
- *Nil:* an explicit multiplier on nil value.

### Side quests

**Primary:** Orange. **Secondary:** Red.

**Most interested:** Bonus Chaser; splashed by Diamond Flood and Heart Chorus.

Side quests set an objective within a round and pay when it is completed.

**Variations.**

- *Objective:* "if you win three hearts tricks, gain X."
- *Chains:* completing one quest reveals the next.
- *Rewards:* points, gold, or a stronger effect for the rest of the round.
- *Run-long:* progress accumulates across rounds.

## Bags

### Bag management

**Primary:** Gray (your bags). **Secondary:** Purple (opponents' bags), Orange
(bags as a resource).

**Most interested:** Contract Attacker, Spade Master, High Card, Bonus Chaser.

Bag mechanics control the cost of overtricks.

**Variations.**

- *Removal:* remove N of your bags.
- *Amnesty:* your overtricks add no bags this round.
- *Waiver:* your next bag penalty is skipped, and your bags carry forward.
- *Punishment:* opponents' bags cost points immediately.
- *Conversion:* gain gold for each bag.

## Economy

### Gold income

**Primary:** Orange. **Secondary:** Blue (gold while held).

**Most interested:** Gold Miner, Diamond Flood, Swap Meet, Blind Bidder.

Gold income buys better sigils and rerolls.

**Variations.**

- *When played:* gain gold when this card is played.
- *While held:* gain gold for each trick this card remains in hand.
- *Conditional:* gain gold when a condition is met.
- *Multipliers:* multiply gold gains.
- *Interest:* raise the interest cap.

### Gold and points conversion

**Primary:** Orange.

**Most interested:** Gold Miner, Diamond Flood.

Conversion links the economy to the score in either direction.

**Variations.**

- *Gold to points:* at the end of each round, convert N gold into points,
  strongest late in the run.
- *Points to gold:* at the end of each round, trade N points for gold,
  strongest early in the run.

### Shop tools

**Primary:** Gray. **Secondary:** Orange.

**Most interested:** every archetype; Gold Miner and Diamond Flood most.

Shop tools help a player find and afford the sigils their deck needs.

**Variations.**

- *Discounts:* reduced prices or free items.
- *Extra offers:* an added sigil in each shop.
- *Rarity:* higher odds of uncommon and rare offers.
- *Rerolls:* cheaper rerolls.
- *Multiple purchases:* buy more than one sigil from a shop.
- *Cards:* buy specific cards for your next opening hand.

### Selling

**Primary:** Gray. **Secondary:** Orange.

**Most interested:** Gold Miner, and any deck changing direction mid-run.

Selling turns sigils back into gold.

**Variations.**

- *Growing value:* this sigil's sell value rises each round.
- *On sale:* gain an effect when this sigil is sold.
- *Recycling:* selling a sigil discounts the next purchase.

## Sigils and engraving

### Engraving control

**Primary:** Gray. **Secondary:** Orange (random engravings).

**Most interested:** While Held, Kingmaker, High Card, and every archetype with
affinity-dependent sigils.

Engraving control places sigils on the cards where they work best.

**Variations.**

- *Choice:* choose which cards receive your sigils.
- *Movement:* when you play this card, move its sigil to another card in your
  hand.
- *Stacking:* engrave two sigils on one card.
- *Random engravings:* engrave random temporary sigils on unengraved cards.

### Duplication and copying

**Primary:** Gray. **Secondary:** Teal (copying other players' sigils).

**Most interested:** every archetype with a key sigil.

Duplication multiplies a collection's best effect.

**Variations.**

- *Duplicate:* a copy of one of your sigils is engraved each round.
- *Copy partner:* copy one of your partner's sigils for the round.
- *Copy opponent:* copy a revealed opponent sigil for the round.

### Disabling sigils

**Primary:** Purple. **Secondary:** Blue.

**Most interested:** Contract Attacker, and any deck facing a known rule setter.

Disabling sigils shuts off opponents' effects.

**Variations.**

- *Targeted:* disable a revealed opponent sigil for the round.
- *Category:* disable all opponents' while-held effects.
- *Card-bound:* the next card an opponent plays has no engraving.

## Run-level progression

### Scaling sigils

**Primary:** Green. **Secondary:** Orange.

**Most interested:** every archetype, especially those bought early.

Scaling sigils grow permanently across the run when their condition is met,
which drives the target growth in round scores.

**Variations.**

- *Counters:* +N contract value each time a condition is met, permanently.
- *Milestones:* the sigil upgrades after N successes.
- *Decay:* the sigil grows each round and resets on a failure.

### Comeback effects

**Primary:** Purple. **Secondary:** Orange.

**Most interested:** Blind Bidder, Contract Attacker.

Comeback effects are stronger while the partnership trails.

**Variations.**

- *Deficit scaling:* rewards grow with the point gap.
- *Threshold:* effects activate when the partnership trails by N points.

### Resonance synergy

**Primary:** each colored resonance counts itself. **Secondary:** Gray counts
variety.

**Most interested:** every archetype.

Resonance synergy rewards commitment to a resonance or to breadth.

**Variations.**

- *Depth:* +N contract value per sigil of this resonance you own, with each
  dual-resonance sigil counting toward both of its resonances.
- *Partnership:* count sigils of this resonance in both partners' collections.
- *Breadth:* a Gray reward for owning sigils of many resonances.
